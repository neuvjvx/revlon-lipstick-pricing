# Revlon Lipstick: Which Shade and Finish to Actually Buy, What a Tube Really Costs, and How to Stop Paying Full Price for It

Revlon lipstick is cheap enough that people buy it in threes, which means the hard part isn't the money. It's the decision. There are roughly 80 shades split across four finishes, the numbering system doesn't tell you which finish you're holding, and the exact same tube is priced at $6.87 on one shelf and $14.27 on another. Pick wrong and the tube lives in a drawer for a year.

So this is a buying guide, not a brand tribute. What the ranges actually are, what current listings charge, which shade numbers keep resurfacing in reviews, and how the price tag itself can be trimmed — including the cashback layer most people never set up before they check out.

## The four finishes — and why the shade number alone doesn't help

Revlon's main lipstick is Super Lustrous, on shelves since 1957. One of its most famous shades, Fire & Ice, launched in 1952, which is a nice illustration of how long this line has been in circulation.

The problem is that a number like 720 or 510 tells you the shade, not the finish. The same numbering pool appears in cream, pearl, matte and shine formats, so you can order "Berry Rich 510" and get a different look depending on which version you grabbed. What's actually on the shelf:

- **Creme** — the classic. Opaque, slight sheen, comfortable to wear.
- **Pearl** — same base with a frosted, pearlescent finish. Reads more early-90s than modern, and shows lip texture more than the creme.
- **The Luscious Mattes** — true matte built with agave, moringa oil and cupuaçu butter, so it doesn't have the drag-and-flake problem.
- **Glass Shine** — a sheer, balm-like gloss finish. This is the one that went viral on TikTok as a candy-apple red.

Revlon's own formula language is consistent across its site and retailer listings: 80% conditioning ingredients, microfine pigments, vitamin E and avocado oil in the creme and pearl versions. The official shade count is a bit elastic — one Revlon page claims 72 "iconic" shades plus 12 new ones, while a Walmart listing describes "over 82" across four finishes. Either way, you're not running out of options.

Decide the finish first, then hunt the shade. Almost every disappointing Revlon purchase comes from doing it the other way round.

## The shade numbers that keep showing up

Some shades have genuine staying power and some are riding a wave. Both are worth knowing about before you order blind.

**Rum Raisin** is the one currently doing the heavy lifting. A Kim Kardashian Instagram post referencing a 1993 photo pushed this deep nude-burgundy shade back into circulation, and it's been the top-selling shade in some markets as a result. It's a creamy, opaque payoff with a satin sheen — halfway between a wine and a rosy brown — and the reason it keeps being described as universally flattering is that it reads differently depending on your skin tone: rosy-brown on one person, brick on another, plummy on a third. It also exists in a Glass Shine balm version and, in liquid form, as ColorStay Overtime in the shade Everlasting Rum.

Beyond that, the frequently cited numbers:

- **720 Fire & Ice** — a bright, hot pink-leaning red. Loud, and it knows it.
- **130 Rose Velvet** — soft rose pink. The safe everyday pick, and a common recommendation for office or school.
- **740 Certainly Red** — a clear, high-energy red. Layers well over 130 if you want to soften it.
- **415 Pink in the Afternoon, 423 Pink Velvet, 435 Love That Pink** — the pink trio people compare against each other. 415 is the sweetest, 423 the most natural, 435 the brightest.
- **125 Nude Velvet, 003 Mauve It Over, 008 Cocoa Craving** — the nude-brown set. 125 for polished-but-neutral, 003 for a soft mauve that flatters teeth, 008 for a deeper cocoa look.
- **Dirty Shirley** — a shimmering coral red introduced more recently, one of the 12 additions.
- **Shine Stealer** (Glass Shine) — glassy, candy-apple red.

One honest caveat that shows up repeatedly in buyer reviews: these shades photograph differently than they wear. A reviewer who called 225 Rosewine a true red got something with orange leanings instead. If a shade is a coin flip for you, the tube is cheap enough that the safer move is buying two rather than agonising.

If transfer is a dealbreaker, the matte or the ColorStay Overtime liquid format is the better fit than the classic creme.

## Questions that come up before buying

**Is Revlon the same quality as the prestige brands?** Reviews don't support a dramatic gap. The recurring praise on the creme and matte lines is pigmentation and comfort; the consistent complaint is transfer, which prestige cream lipsticks share. What you're paying for at a higher price point is usually packaging, shade curation and marketing.

**Does the matte formula dry out your lips?** The Luscious Mattes is built differently from the older drugstore mattes — the botanical complex is there specifically to prevent that. Prep still matters: balm on, blot off, then apply.

**How do I stop the colour from fading at the centre?** Apply a thin layer, blot with tissue, then apply a second. Setting the edge with a dusting of powder helps if you're wearing a mask.

**What if the cashback never tracks?** File a missing cashback claim from inside your account with the order number, date and subtotal. Support checks tracking records against the retailer's data.
